# About Me

I am a cognitive neuroscientist and researcher interested in how technology can support multilingual learners and strengthen the active use of minority and heritage languages. My work explores how children and families engage with AI-powered learning tools, including interactive storybooks, conversational agents, and speech technologies.

I bring a background in developmental science, human-computer interaction, cognitive neuroscience, and bilingual education. Across my work, I focus on technologies that are inclusive of diverse language backgrounds, grounded in research, and designed with families and educators in mind.

## Research Journey

I completed my Ph.D. in Cognitive Neuroscience at the Basque Center on Cognition, Brain, and Language, where I studied how accented speech influences attention and memory using EEG and behavioral methods. At the University of Michigan and Harvard Graduate School of Education, I extended this work to examine how children interact with AI storytelling characters and speech technologies.

What began as an interest in how conversational partners shape listening and memory has grown into a broader focus on how people speak, understand, trust, and learn in a multilingual, increasingly technology-mediated world.

## Community Engagement

I care deeply about making science accessible. I have been involved in community-facing science activities including Pint of Science, Brain Awareness Week, immigrant-support networks in Donostia, and emerging work around community AI literacy for families and educators.

If you are organizing local science outreach, bilingual education programming, or responsible AI learning resources, I would be glad to connect.
